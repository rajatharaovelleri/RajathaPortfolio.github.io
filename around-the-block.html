01 / Project overview

Good places.
Hard to find.

08Observation participants
08Interview participants
01Mobile product concept

People often want to support independent businesses, but when they need something specific, the quickest search doesn't always lead them to a local shop.

Around the Block is my individual product-design exploration of that gap. I designed Local Market, a mobile concept that brings neighborhood businesses, product information, reviews, store stories, and maps into a clearer discovery experience.

The design direction builds on findings from a DePaul University research study of how people discover and choose local businesses.

02 / The opportunity
“What if finding a local shop felt as effortless as choosing a familiar chain?”
01 / DISCOVERY

Local doesn’t always mean independent.

Participants often found nearby stores through search engines, but those results did not reliably distinguish independent businesses from chains.

02 / CONFIDENCE

Information shapes decisions.

Store hours, pricing, product details, real photos and reviews helped people decide whether a new business was worth visiting.

03 / CONNECTION

People want more than a transaction.

Stories, expertise and community connection made independent businesses appealing even when large retailers were more convenient.

03 / Research & discovery

Before designing,
understand why.

Supporting local businesses sounds simple. Finding the right one rarely is. The research explored how people search for nearby independent shops, what makes them trust a listing, and why convenience often wins over intention.

01 / Contextual observation
08

Watch the search unfold

Eight adults, ages 21–44, were observed searching for a local store or specialty grocery product using their usual tools. Sessions took approximately 15–20 minutes and included follow-up questions about choices and frustrations.

04 / From insight to interface

Research gave
me the why.
Design became
the how.

I translated the research into an individual mobile product concept: a simpler way to discover independent businesses, understand what they offer, and decide whether to visit or get in touch. Each major feature answers a specific need identified in the study.

Design challenge / How might I…

Help shoppers discover independent neighborhood businesses and make confident decisions without piecing information together across multiple apps?

01 / FIND

Make discovery local-first

Put neighborhood businesses, search, categories, filters, and a map within easy reach. This responds to participants' difficulty distinguishing independent stores from familiar chains.

02 / EVALUATE

Make details easy to trust

Bring store hours, location, photos, reviews, product descriptions, and prices into a consistent business profile so people can compare options without switching services.

03 / RETURN

Keep the connection going

Let shoppers save businesses, explore their stories, and contact a store about pickup. The prototype intentionally stops short of claiming a completed purchase.

06 / Reflection & next steps

Designing for
the details.

The most important lesson from this exploration is that supporting local businesses should not require extra effort from the shopper. Good intentions are not enough when information is scattered or unreliable.

Instead of adding more features, I focused on connecting the essentials: discovery, credible store details, products, and a clear next step.

01 / What I learned

Trust is built through small details: accurate hours, clear prices, authentic photos and transparent store information. These were recurring needs in the research.

02 / What I would test

Run moderated task-based usability sessions to see whether people can find a specific product, identify an independent store, save it and reach a contact option without confusion.

03 / What I would improve

Validate search terminology and filters, design real profile settings, and explore verified business information and availability updates with store owners.
